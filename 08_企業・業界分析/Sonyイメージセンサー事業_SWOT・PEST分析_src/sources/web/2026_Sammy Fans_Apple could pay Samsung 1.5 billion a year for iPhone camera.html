Apple could pay Samsung $1.5 billion a year for iPhone camera

Published

9 months ago

on

January 11, 2026

By

Yash Rathore
  • Share
  • Tweet

After nearly a decade of keeping Samsung at arm’s length, Apple has signed a new iPhone camera deal with Samsung Foundry. This time, it is not about processors. It is about the camera, arguably the most visible part of the iPhone.

SemiAnalysis (via Jukan) estimates Samsung could secure 20 to 30 percent of Apple iPhone total CIS volume by 2027. It translates to roughly 150 to 200 million sensors per year and around $1 billion to $1.5 billion in annual revenue.

Samsung Foundry will manufacture advanced CMOS Image Sensors for future iPhones at its Austin, Texas facility. The agreement breaks Sony’s long-standing exclusivity as Apple’s sole image sensor supplier.

The Korean tech giant is expected to produce sensors using a sophisticated three-stack wafer-to-wafer hybrid bonding process. The sensors will deliver better light capture, faster readout speeds, and more room for on-sensor processing.

Production is expected to begin around March 2026, suggesting these sensors could land in iPhones as early as the iPhone 18 lineup. Sony, until now, owned the camera sensor side.

By bringing Samsung Foundry into the image sensor pipeline, Apple reduces risk while keeping its most advanced logic manufacturing tied to TSMC.

The Taiwanese Foundry giant handles nearly all of Apple’s advanced logic chips, from A-series processors to custom silicon across Macs and Vision products.

Samsung’s Galaxy Z Fold 8 series is gaining another round of recognition from independent consumer testing bodies, this time across five major European markets.

According to the information (via FNNews), the Galaxy Z Fold 8 and Fold 8 Ultra have secured top positions in foldable smartphone evaluations conducted by consumer magazines in Italy, Spain, Portugal, the Netherlands, and Belgium.

Independent organizations including Altroconsumo, OCU, DECO PROTESTE, Consumentenbond, and Test-Achats conducted the evaluations. These publications assess products through their own testing methods, covering areas such as performance, durability, and everyday usability.

The standard Galaxy Z Fold 8 scored 81 points to take first place in Italy, Spain, and Portugal, while the Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra followed closely with 80 points. In the Netherlands and Belgium, both models shared the top position.

The Galaxy Z Fold 8 and Fold 8 Ultra also received the “Best of Test” distinction in four of the five countries, highlighting their overall test performance.

Android flagships could equip 10,000mAh steel-cased battery thanks to Apple

Published

18 hours ago

on

September 26, 2026

By

Yash Rathore

We may soon welcome a flagship Android phone equipped with a 10,000mAh steel-cased battery. Apple has reportedly paved the way for its rivals, with Chinese manufacturers targeting a massive bump in terms of battery capacity.

After Chinese phone makers pushed flagship battery capacities beyond 6,000mAh with silicon-carbon technology, a new leak points to another major jump: 9,000mAh to 10,000mAh batteries using steel casing and stacked-cell construction.

10,000mAh steel-cased battery phones are coming

Leaker DCS reports that a major Chinese manufacturer is testing this combination for future flagship phones. The identity has not been confirmed, although Xiaomi is considered a likely candidate based on the info shared by the leaker.

Apple moved to an aluminum-encased battery with the iPhone 16 Pro and has continued developing the approach. The iPhone 18 Pro Max now has a confirmed 5,391mAh rated battery capacity in models with a physical SIM tray, representing an increase over the previous generation.

Digital Chat Station’s latest disclosure suggests Android manufacturers are testing steel rather than simply copying Apple’s earlier aluminum approach. Combined with stacked-cell construction, this could allow manufacturers to make better use of limited internal volume.

Stacked cells arrange the battery’s internal layers differently from conventional rolled designs. The technology can improve space utilization and energy density, making it particularly interesting for phones that already have demanding displays, cameras, and powerful processors.

Samsung, meanwhile, is preparing a big battery boost for the Galaxy S27 Pro and Galaxy S27 Ultra. The company is expected to launch the new flagship phones with 5,200mAh and 5,700mAh battery cells, respectively.

HSBC UK banking app no longer works inside Secure Folder on Android phones

Published

1 day ago

on

September 26, 2026

By

Sheetal Malviya

HSBC UK has stopped its banking app from working inside Samsung’s Secure Folder and Android’s Private Space. The change appears to have happened suddenly, and some users are unhappy because they were not informed earlier.

Samsung Secure Folder is a feature that keeps apps and personal information in a separate and protected area of the phone. Many people use it for banking apps because it adds another layer of security.

However, HSBC UK has decided that its app will no longer work inside these protected spaces. The same restriction also applies to Android Private Space, which offers a similar feature on some Android phones.

HSBC says the change is related to security. The bank says using its app inside Secure Folder or Private Space could make it harder to detect some security threats. It could also affect the bank’s ability to send fraud alerts to customers quickly.

It is currently unclear whether HSBC has introduced the same restriction in other countries. So far, the change has been confirmed for HSBC UK.

The decision has caused frustration among some customers. They point out that other banking apps still work inside Samsung Secure Folder. Some users are also unhappy that HSBC did not clearly warn them about the change before it happened.

Advertisement

For customers affected by the change, the HSBC app can still be used normally, but it cannot be used inside Samsung Secure Folder or Android Private Space.

Samsung wants to make money from SmartThings

Published

1 day ago

on

September 26, 2026

By

Yash Rathore

Samsung is reportedly exploring ways to make money from SmartThings. The company’s SmartThings platform has already exceeded 460 million registered users, and it seems to be ready for monetization at scale.

Korean outlet GEnews reports that Samsung appears to be exploring how its SmartThings can earn money directly, while also using connectivity to make Galaxy and home appliances more attractive to existing users.

The Korean tech giant is preparing a broader monetization strategy for SmartThings. The platform had more than 460 million registered users worldwide as of June, giving Samsung a huge installed base to build new services around.

Samsung plans to introduce a paid commercial API pricing model in October. External companies could use SmartThings connectivity to manage devices and access information for services such as hotel room controls or energy management.

Samsung SmartThings is evolving

Samsung has increasingly connected smartphones, TVs, refrigerators, robot vacuum cleaners and other appliances through SmartThings. However, features such as Family Care also show where Samsung sees additional value.

Samsung is also bringing its personalized Now Brief experience to supported home appliances. Users will be able to see information such as device status and energy consumption on compatible TVs and Family Hub refrigerators.
